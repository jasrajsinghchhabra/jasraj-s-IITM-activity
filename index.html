Statistics – 1

Describing data, measuring uncertainty and reasoning from samples.

Course description

Statistics – 1 is an introductory course in the Term 1 of the BS in Data Science and Applications at IIT Madras. It teaches how to describe and summarise data, study the relationship between two variables, count possibilities, and use probability to reason about uncertainty.

Learning objectives

Understand data

Identify types of data and measurement scales, and tell descriptive methods from inferential ones.

Describe data

Summarise categorical and numerical data using tables, graphs, measures of centre and spread, and the five-number summary.

Relate variables

Analyse association between two variables using contingency tables, scatterplots, covariance and correlation.

Count possibilities

Use the addition and multiplication rules, factorials, permutations and combinations.

Use probability

Work with sample spaces, events and the probability axioms.

Reason conditionally

Apply conditional probability, independence, the law of total probability and Bayes’ theorem.

Why statistics matters in data science

  • Understanding data. Summary measures and charts reveal patterns and problems before any modelling.
  • Handling uncertainty. Probability and inference tell us how far to trust a result drawn from a sample.
  • Building models. Regression, classification and many machine learning methods rest on statistical foundations.
  • Testing ideas. Hypothesis testing and experiments separate real effects from chance.
  • Avoiding wrong conclusions. Statistical thinking guards against bias, small samples and confusing correlation with causation.

My learning reflection

Studying Statistics – 1 has shown me that data on its own does not answer questions; it has to be summarised and interpreted carefully. Ideas such as measures of centre and spread, and later counting and probability, gave me a way to describe variation and uncertainty instead of ignoring them.

The idea I find most interesting is how a few simple numbers, such as the mean and the quartiles, can summarise an entire dataset. The part I found most challenging is keeping the many definitions clear and choosing the right measure or rule for each kind of data.

I now see statistics as the base for data science: before using any tool, I should be able to explain what the numbers mean and how certain I can be about them. Going forward I want to keep practising with examples, so that I can explain what the numbers mean and how certain I can be about them.
